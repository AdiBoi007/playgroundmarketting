## Proposed traction copy — verify before publishing

| Draft claim | What the figure must describe |
| --- | --- |
| “2,000+ people in our pilot customer community.” | At least 2,000 real, distinct community members, with availability described accurately. This does not mean 2,000 people have completed a test. |
| “50+ industries and domains.” | A defined set of distinct industries and domains covered by verified participant backgrounds. Examples such as tech, fashion, B2B and finance describe the intended breadth; they are not measured counts or company partnerships. |

The suggested figures came from a brainstorming request, not measured site data.

## Concrete offers worth establishing next

- “Your first pilot customers in [X] business days.” Define when the clock starts and which audiences qualify.
- “Pilot tests from $[X].” State currency and what the starting price includes.
- “[N] relevant participants per test.” Confirm the promised audience and participant count before offering it.

A real customer quote or a short result from a completed test can replace additional generic sales copy once permission and supporting details are available.

## Customer-reported growth result

On 19 October 2026, the founder reported that people had gone from no growth to 80% growth in one month because of connections on Playground. The Why Playground comparison uses only these supplied values: 0% before and 80% after, with customer-reported attribution.

The founder subsequently specified that the 80% one-month growth applies to revenue, paying customers and total users. A single comparison chart shows all three reported growth rates on the same 0–100% scale. Company identity, exact dates and underlying records have not been supplied; no company name or logo is attached to this result. It does not present the result as an average, a controlled causal finding, or a guaranteed outcome. The former fictional counts of 12 pilot customers and 4 of 5 questions tested have been removed from this section.
